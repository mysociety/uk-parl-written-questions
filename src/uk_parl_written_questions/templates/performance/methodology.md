**Answered on time:** the number of questions that received a substantive answer on or before their response target. This is a subset of **Answered**, which includes both on-time and late answers.

**On-schedule rate (%):** Answered on time ÷ (Answered + Overdue) × 100. This measures the proportion answered on time among questions that have either received a substantive answer or passed their response target. Late answers and unanswered overdue questions count against the rate. Questions still awaiting an answer within their response window (**Pending**) are excluded. If every question is pending, or there are no questions, we show a dash instead of a percentage.

For example, if 23 questions have been answered, 9 of those were answered on time, and 24 remain overdue, the on-schedule rate is 9 ÷ (23 + 24) × 100 = 19.1%.

**Ordinary questions:** the published date for answer is the earliest date a formal answer can be given. We use the fifth working day on or after that date as the response target, counting the date itself as day one when it is a working day. For example, a question tabled on Monday with a Wednesday date for answer has a target of the following Tuesday, assuming no bank holidays.

**Urgent (named day) questions:** the response target is the specified answer date. A substantive answer received on the target date counts as on time. Holding replies do not count as substantive answers; those questions remain pending until their response target passes, then count as overdue.

Working days exclude weekends and England and Wales bank holidays. We do not 
otherwise exclude parliamentary recesses. 

This follows the worked example in the [Table Office’s evidence (Q144)](https://committees.parliament.uk/oralevidence/17713/pdf/#page=15) and [Government evidence (paragraph 27)](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/161782/html/). The Table Office also describes a broader ten-working-day standard; this dashboard uses the five-working-day target.

Withdrawn questions and non-government answering bodies are excluded.
